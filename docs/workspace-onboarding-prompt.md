# Workspace onboarding prompt

Send this to any Claude chat that knows a business well (the Remedae chat,
the Her Comeback Club chat, and so on). Paste the answer into Settings ->
Knowledge -> "Paste a dossier" in the copilot, or hand it to the copilot
session and it will embed it. The headings are fixed so the import maps
them; keep them exactly.

---

You are writing the onboarding dossier for a business so that an operating team can be briefed on it consistently. Use only what you actually know from this conversation and its files. Where you do not know something, write UNKNOWN rather than guessing. Facts, not marketing. British English. Never use em dashes or en dashes. Plain markdown, no tables, no preamble, under 1,800 words. Use exactly these thirteen headings, in this order, each as a level-two heading:

## Business
What it is in one paragraph: the company, its arms or product lines, its stage (idea, pre-launch, launched, revenue), how it makes or will make money, and the founding intent.

## Audience
Who it is for, in specifics: the person, their situation, what they are trying to do, where they are found, and who it is not for.

## Offers and pricing
Every product, service, programme or tier that exists or is planned, with price, what is included, and whether it is live, in build, or an idea.

## Current state of play
What actually exists today: website and app pages that are live, what is published, channels and their rough size, what is in progress, what is broken or blocked, recent milestones with dates.

## Strategy and next 90 days
Positioning in one sentence, the current priorities in order, the goals for the next ninety days, and the single most important thing.

## Decisions already made
Choices that are settled and must not be reopened: naming, positioning calls, pricing calls, things tried and rejected, and why. One line each.

## Clients and proof
Past or current clients, case studies, testimonials, results, partnerships, anything that counts as proof.

## Team and partners
Who is involved, their roles, and external partners or suppliers worth knowing.

## Market
The market, the alternatives a customer would consider, named competitors and how this differs, relevant trends.

## Technical
The stack, repositories and where they live, hosting, integrations, publishing contracts (how content or data gets onto the live product), environments, and anything an engineer or designer must not break.

## Compliance and risks
Legal, regulatory and claims constraints (for a health business: what may and may not be said), data handling, contracts, known risks.

## Links
Website, app, Instagram and other channels, repositories, documents, spreadsheets, design files. One per line as label: URL.

## Open questions
What this dossier could not answer and who could.
