# Should Remedae state dose amounts? Seat advice, 14 September 2026

Founder question: should the product suggest dose amounts for things not specified as a home remedy, while staying compliant as an educational tool, not a diagnosis tool.

TLDR from the seats: No. Report what traditions and studies used, never instruct an amount, except kitchen quantities inside a home recipe.

- Traditional medicine seat: kitchen remedies carry their dose in the recipe (half a lemon, 250ml, ten minutes) and stripping that falsifies the tradition. Everything formula shaped is practitioner dispensed in the traditions themselves: say so, give no number. The September card failures (adult ashwagandha doses on child cards, self dosed Rauwolfia) are what blurring this looks like.
- Modern medicine seat: a milligram number is an instruction whatever the prose. Nothing prescription only or pharmacy only carries any dose from us, not even study doses. "Reported in studies as" only protects when the whole page reads as a literature summary with study, year, population and a "not a dose for you" line. Paediatrics absolute: no dose of anything implies safety for a child. Differs from the traditional seat in allowing reported adult study ranges for well studied supplements behind strict framing.
- Chief scientific officer: the deciding distinction is preparation versus pharmacology. Kitchen quantity in a recipe's steps is preparation, publish it. A mg, g or granule figure attached to a substance is pharmacology, report it in research context only, never in steps, titles or the imperative. Dose is exactly where an educational tool quietly becomes a treatment tool.
- Counsel (not a solicitor): dosing instructions are the strongest single signal for both the MHRA medicinal-product borderline and the software-as-device line; the CAP Code separately bars advertising POMs to the public. Have a regulatory solicitor sanity check before marketing scales.

## Recommended dosing policy (not yet adopted; awaiting the founder)
1. Recipe cards may state kitchen quantities and timings only (ml, cups, halves, minutes).
2. No card ever states a pharmacological dose as an instruction: no mg, g, IU, granule or tablet counts in steps or titles.
3. Study doses may appear only in the research pane, past tense, with study, year and population.
4. Prescription only and pharmacy medicines: no dose anywhere, including study doses; name the class, route to pharmacist or GP.
5. Practitioner dispensed formulas: named, described, "dose set by a qualified practitioner", no number.
6. Paediatric cards: no quantities of any active substance; preparation only, age floor, "speak to a pharmacist or GP".
7. Fixed educational disclaimer on every card; no imperative dose language.
8. Any card failing 1 to 7 is blocked at the schema gate, not fixed by hand.
