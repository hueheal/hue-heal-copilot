# Remedae research-gap register

The standing record of conditions where Remedae searched the traditional literature and found no credible, documented remedy in any system. Each entry is shown honestly on its live page ("The traditions are quiet on this one"), collects reader interest through the shelf-{slug} notify list, and is raised with the bodies below. The scientist seat owns this file; add entries when a page ships with zero traditional panes, retire them when a credible source lands.

## Who we flag gaps to

Chosen per docs/sources.md in the remedae repo. Outreach is the founder's send, from admin@remedae.app, using the template at the end.

- WHO Traditional Medicine (the team behind ICD-11 chapter 26 / TM2): the global registry where traditional diagnoses meet modern coding.
- NCCIH (US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health): the largest funder of trials on traditional and complementary interventions.
- CCRAS and the AYUSH research councils (Ayurveda, Unani via CCRUM, Siddha via CCRS): commissioning bodies for the Indian systems.
- JSOM and the Kampo research community (with PMDA-listed formulas as the evidence base).
- WNF (World Naturopathic Federation) research committee.
- Cochrane Complementary Medicine field: for prioritising reviews where primary trials exist but no synthesis does.

## Open gaps

### G6PD deficiency (live, 16 September 2026)
No traditional system has credible literature on managing G6PD deficiency. The critical framing: this is the one condition class where trying traditional remedies is itself the hazard, since many herbs and supplements can trigger haemolysis. The research ask is therefore not "trial a remedy" but "publish tradition-facing safety guidance": which classical materia medica are oxidative triggers. That guidance does not exist in indexed literature and would protect patients across every system.
Reader interest: shelf-g6pd notify list, plus one reader served by the condition-ready email flow.

### Geographic tongue (draft, 16 September 2026)
Benign, prevalent (roughly 1 to 3 percent), and essentially unstudied beyond epidemiology. No management guideline exists in modern literature either; the only RCT is small (low-level laser, n=50). A pragmatic trial of reassurance plus trigger avoidance versus usual care would settle the everyday advice. No credible traditional literature found.
