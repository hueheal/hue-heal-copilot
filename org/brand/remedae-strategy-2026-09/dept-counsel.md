# Counsel review: Remedae strategy, September 2026

I am not a solicitor. Where I say solicitor or accountant, use one before you rely on it.

## What still holds

- "Educational library, not a medical device" is right and the live product supports it: no diagnosis, no personalisation, no verdicts, a practitioner advisory and "Educational. Not medical advice." on every page. The protection rests on pages staying generic. Once the product reads a user's own data and answers about them, MHRA software-as-a-medical-device guidance applies.
- Evidence honesty and the sources.md citation rule are exactly what the CAP Code asks for. Keep it as house style for every public claim.
- The financial plan's hygiene is right: advance assurance first, PI and cyber insurance, legal budget, projections withdrawn.

## Claims to fix in the deck and plan

Each rewrite keeps what Experts say is true today.

1. "240+ conditions" (deck, plan, review budget). Rewrite: "105 published conditions, 240 mapped".
2. "The product is complete and launches at remedae.com in August 2026". Rewrite: "Live at remedae.app since August 2026 behind a waitlist gate".
3. "The library public, medically reviewed" (ask slide). No advisor named. Rewrite: "medically reviewed once the first reviewer is in place".
4. "Saved remedies, protocols, interaction checking" (plan, deck). Rewrite: "Saved remedies, interaction notes on every remedy". Drop "family profiles" until decided.
5. "The first consumer platform built on [ICD-11 TM]" and "the most trusted place on the internet". Superlatives need substantiation. Rewrite: "built on ICD-11's traditional medicine modules" and "built to be trusted" (already on the cover).
6. "45+ governing bodies mapped and tiered. The pipeline is specified, not imagined". Only PubMed, WHO ICD-11 and the curated corpus are ingested; most bodies are web reference only, and naming WHO, AYUSH and PMDA can imply endorsement, which sources.md disclaims. Rewrite: "45+ governing bodies and datasets catalogued; three ingested today, the rest tiered. No endorsement implied."
7. "Your nan was right" implies the remedies work. Fine as investor narrative; in public marketing it is an implied health claim. Consumer line: "Your nan had her reasons. Here is what the evidence says."
8. "Every remedy graded on evidence". True for the published 105. Add "published".
9. Comparables (Calm, Headspace, Noom, MasterClass). Keep, with "context, not projection" on the same slide.
10. Revenue share "50/50 to 70/30". Say "target" until an agreement exists.
