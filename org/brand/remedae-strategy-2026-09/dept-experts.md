# Experts department review of the July 2026 strategy

Chief scientific officer, with the modern medicine and pharmacology expert and the traditional medicine practitioner. Read against the facts of 8 September 2026. Nothing here is individual advice.

## What still holds

- The honesty position is the moat: educational library, not a clinic; contested traditions labelled as contested; no verdicts, no diagnosis.
- The pipeline principle is sound. Nothing reaches a page unless it traces to a named body and a verbatim passage. The quote verifier and "skip beats fabricate" are the right architecture.
- Traditional view: 13 traditions at equal dignity, graded separately, is correct. "Used for" is never "proven for".
- Modern view: mandatory safety fields and a safety floor that can never render empty. Keep both.

## What is now wrong or stale

- "240+ conditions, medically reviewed" cannot be said. 105 are live and none has been externally reviewed. Say: "105 conditions, source-verified by an automated pipeline, clinical review beginning [date]". Until one reviewer has signed one page, "reviewed" is a misrepresentation and Counsel should treat it as such.
- The corpus figure (173) is stale; 786 is true.
- "Product complete" and "medically reviewable at scale" describe a pipeline. A 40% historic failure rate on live generation and a 0.85 confidence floor are triage, not review.
- NICE CKS is named in ingestion.md as a modern source. It is not licensed for commercial reuse. Any passage distilled from CKS must be re-sourced to NICE guidelines (NG series) or NHS.uk before launch.
- "AI can hold nuance" about interactions describes a feature that does not exist. The deck and Remedae+ both promise interaction checking; nothing in the repo does it.
- Review is budgeted at £150 for 240 conditions. With 105 live the sweep is £15,750, but the August to March timeline has already slipped.

## Evidence and honesty position

The five-point scale is a provenance scale, not an evidence-grading methodology. Levels 1 to 3 say where a claim came from; 4 and 5 say how good the trials are. A Kampo formula with PMDA approval and one old RCT lands ambiguously between 3 and 5. The data room promises a methodology; none has been written.

Fix: two marks. A traditional-use mark (classical text, living regulator, folk use) and an evidence mark aligned loosely to GRADE (high, moderate, low, very low, none). The verbatim-quote verifier supports the second only if the extractor also captures study type, sample size and outcome. Today it captures numbers only when they appear in the quote: necessary, not sufficient.

Homeopathy: the current wording ("contested evidence", NHMRC 2015, NHS England 2017) is honest. Policy: every homeopathy pane carries a fixed sentence that the major systematic reviews find no effect beyond placebo, is never listed first, and carries no evidence mark above "very low". The practitioner adds: describe it accurately and without contempt.

## Medical review plan

At £150 a credentialed reviewer gives 60 to 90 minutes: read every pane, check safety fields against the BNF and EMA monographs, confirm the modern pane against a guideline, flag anything unsafe in a tradition pane. That is a safety and accuracy review, not a systematic evidence review, and the site should say "clinically reviewed for safety and accuracy".

Who: GPhC-registered pharmacists for interactions and dose; a GP or nurse prescriber for the modern pane; a registered medical herbalist (NIMH or CPP) for herbal panes. Recruit through the Royal Pharmaceutical Society, NIMH and the College of Medicine network. One named clinical advisor signs the process, not every page.

Process: reviewer sees the page and its source passages, signs a short checklist per pane; the page shows reviewer name, registration body and date. Re-review on any source change and every 24 months. Start with the 30 highest-traffic conditions and every women's health, paediatric and pregnancy-relevant page.

## Research agenda

The ingestion log of "skip" results across 105 conditions is already a map of thin evidence per tradition. Publish it. That is the honest version of "Remedae maps the gap".

Women's health as Phase 3 flagship is credible: the gap is measurable and the remedies (ginger, chasteberry, cramp bark, black seed, shatavari) are widely used. A realistic first partnership is not WHO. It is one academic group already in the field: a scoping review co-authored with a UK school of pharmacy or women's health research unit, using Remedae's corpus as the ethnobotanical dataset. A paper opens the institutional door.
