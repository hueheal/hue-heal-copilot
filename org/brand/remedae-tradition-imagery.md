# The one image per tradition

Compiled 10 September 2026 from the imagery guide (v1.2), the tradition content on the site, and an external research pass on how each tradition is practised today (`review/imagery/tradition-research.md`, every claim sourced). The rule from the guide holds throughout: the tradition shows in the object and the gesture, never in dress or décor, and the shot must read as someone's actual Tuesday this year.

Each entry gives the house shot, why it is the honest one, two alternates, and what stays out of frame. Ready prompts for Higgsfield are in `content/imagery/tradition-shots.json`, composed from the library's master prompt, the tradition module and the subject line below.

## Modern medicine

**The shot (hands-object).** A white pharmacy prescription bag being opened on a kitchen table, one blister strip and a glass of water beside it, morning light from a window, hands only.

**Why.** The dispensed prescription is the everyday form of modern medicine in the UK: 1.26 billion items in 2024/25 (NHSBSA) against 31.9 million GP appointments a month. The white bag is instantly read and nobody needs a caption. Home, after a practitioner.

**Alternates.** Pharmacy First counter: a pharmacist's hands passing a small box across the counter, no uniform detail in focus; A sleep diary and pen on a bedside table.

**Never.** stethoscope on a white coat, corridors, pills spilling, monitors.

## Ayurveda

**The shot (hands-object).** A copper tongue scraper held in a hand at a bathroom sink, morning light, a steel cup of warm water on the ledge, no face.

**Why.** The morning routine (dinacharya) is the most lived Ayurveda in diaspora homes; the tongue scraper is the object users recognise first and non-users can still read. Ayurveda is the most used AYUSH system in India (NSSO 2022 to 2023) and in the UK is kitchen and routine, not clinic. Home.

**Alternates.** Turmeric stirred into warm milk in a small steel pan; Ghee spooned into a pan, a spice tin open beside it.

**Never.** yoga poses as a stand-in, Om, Ganesha or Buddha statues as décor (South Asian practitioners have objected), temples, kurta.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine

**The shot (macro-body).** Three fingers resting on the inside of a wrist at a clinic desk, both hands in frame, a plain sleeve, daylight from the side, faces out of frame.

**Why.** The 60 to 75 minute first consultation with both-wrist pulse diagnosis is what every UK TCM patient experiences (RCHM). It is the one gesture that is unmistakably TCM and involves no décor. Practitioner.

**Alternates.** A foil sachet of spray-dried granules stirred into a mug of hot water; Goji and jujube on a chopping board.

**Never.** dragons, calligraphy walls, apothecary drawers as set dressing.

## Kampo

**The shot (hands-object).** A numbered foil stick of extract granules torn open and tipped into a small glass of water on a plain kitchen counter, hands only, bright daylight.

**Why.** Kampo today is a licensed pharmaceutical: 148 formulations on Japan's insurance price list, prescribed by around 90 percent of physicians, dispensed as single-dose aluminium sachets (PMC3914391). The torn stick is the honest image; the apothecary drawer is not. Home, after a practitioner.

**Alternates.** The same sachet on a pharmacy counter with a glass of water; Ginger and jujube on a wooden board.

**Never.** kimono, ceramic drawers, brush-written labels, temples.

## Unani

**The shot (hands-object).** A teaspoon of honey with black seeds stirred through it, held over a small glass bowl, a kitchen worktop soft behind, morning light.

**Why.** Black seed with honey is the daily Unani practice in British Muslim households, and joshanda sachets have been a commercial product for about 70 years (Science Museum Group). Both are home objects a reader can buy this week. Home.

**Alternates.** A joshanda sachet torn over a steaming mug; Almonds soaking overnight in a glass.

**Never.** Quranic text or prayer imagery as décor, kaftan.

## Siddha

**The shot (hands-object).** A steel tumbler of dark brown nilavembu decoction held in one hand, a steel pan and strainer soft behind on a hob, daylight.

**Why.** Nilavembu kudineer is the Siddha remedy people actually take: a bought nine-herb powder boiled at home, handed out by the Tamil Nadu government in dengue seasons (Scroll.in), and kabasura kudineer had a trial during COVID (PMC8441246). The steel tumbler is the recognisable object. Home.

**Alternates.** Kudineer powder measured from a packet into a pan; Fresh herbs on a banana leaf beside a mortar.

**Never.** shrines, temple imagery.

## Naturopathy

**The shot (macro-body).** A hand under a running shower head switched to cold, water beading on the forearm, tiles soft behind, bright bathroom light.

**Why.** Contrast hydrotherapy is one of the six naturopathic pillars (WNF) and the one a reader can do tonight without buying anything. The bought form is a THR-marked herbal box or a bespoke tincture, both harder to read at a glance. Home.

**Alternates.** A dropper of bitter tincture over a glass of water; A steam bowl under a towel, hands either side.

**Never.** herb bundles as a cauldron cliché, spa towels, orchids.

## Homeopathy

**The shot (hands-object).** Small white pillules tipped from a brown glass vial into its cap, held in a palm, a plain table soft behind, daylight.

**Why.** The pillule vial is the everyday form: Nelsons Arnica 30c at Boots, Helios and Ainsworths vials from GPhC-registered homeopathic pharmacies. The image must not promise more than the page does; NHS England ended funding by 2023, and the Society of Homeopaths lost PSA accreditation in 2021. Home.

**Alternates.** A row of small brown bottles on a pharmacy shelf, labels out of focus; A practitioner listening across an ordinary desk.

**Never.** NHS branding, anything implying licensed efficacy.

## African traditional medicine

**The shot (hands-object).** A bunch of fresh bitter leaf being rinsed under a kitchen tap, water on the leaves, hands only, bright daylight.

**Why.** Bitter leaf is bought weekly from Peckham and Brixton grocers for soups and herbal preparations (africanstores.co.uk; Niyis), and agbo decoctions are the most used preparations in Lagos surveys (PMC3252251). The plant and the pot are the honest image; masks turn people into types (Cleveland Museum of Art). Home.

**Alternates.** Rooibos steeping in a glass mug; A bitter kola nut split on a board.

**Never.** masks, ceremony, poverty framing, tribal styling.

## Indigenous healing

**The shot (hands-object).** A small tin of kawakawa balm open on a windowsill, a fingertip taking a little, leaves soft behind the glass, daylight.

**Why.** Kawakawa balm and tea in rongoā Māori (Te Papa; University of Auckland) and Bush Medijina balms made by Anindilyakwa women (indigenous.gov.au) are forms communities present publicly as everyday. Te Papa notes rongoā knowledge was tapu; ceremonial objects, sage bundles and regalia are closed and are not photographed. Home, community-made.

**Alternates.** Eucalyptus leaves in a bowl of steaming water; A brand texture: sunlight through leaves onto a wall.

**Never.** ceremonies, pipes, feathers, medicine pouches, white sage smudge bundles, headdresses.

## Functional medicine

**The shot (hands-object).** A finger-prick blood test kit laid out on a kitchen table beside a food and symptom diary and a coffee, hands filling in the diary, morning light.

**Why.** The at-home blood panel (Medichecks) and the three-week elimination and reintroduction diet (IFM) are the two everyday forms; the diary is the object that shows the method. BANT practitioners sit on the CNHC register. Home under practitioner guidance.

**Alternates.** A reintroduction diary with one food circled; A consultation over a laptop in a bright room.

**Never.** white-coat lab clichés, supplement walls.

## Lifestyle medicine

**The shot (environmental).** One person in a coat walking a canal path after lunch, phone in a pocket, bright overcast daylight, three-quarter length from behind.

**Why.** BSLM's six pillars are eating, mental wellbeing, relationships, activity, substances and sleep; the post-meal walk is the pillar that photographs as a Tuesday rather than a campaign. Home-enacted.

**Alternates.** A phone charging on a hallway table outside a dark bedroom; A kettle switched off, the last coffee cup rinsed.

**Never.** smoothie bowls, gym mirrors, activewear campaigns.

## Mind-body medicine

**The shot (macro-body).** A hand flat on the chest and one on the belly over a plain tee, seated on an ordinary chair by a window, eyes closed, cropped at the chin.

**Why.** Eight-week MBCT groups are in NICE NG222 and delivered inside NHS Talking Therapies (Lancet Psychiatry 2025); what the reader takes home is breath as a body practice. The hands show the practice without a pose. Home, after a taught course.

**Alternates.** A community hall with mismatched chairs, one person seated, others out of focus; A pen and a worry list on a bedside table at night.

**Never.** lotus position on a beach, prayer hands, meditation cushions as décor, sacred symbols as décor.

## Reading the set together

Nine of the thirteen are hands and an object on a kitchen or bathroom surface, which is deliberate: it is where these traditions live in a UK home today. Two are practitioner gestures (TCM pulse, mind-body hands as taught in a course), one is outdoors (lifestyle), one is a home first-response (naturopathy). Shot at the same focal length, light and grade, they read as one shelf, which is the point of the site.

Gaps the research could not close: no source describes Siddha practice in the UK, and no UK-specific study of African diaspora herbal use exists, so those two shots rest on retail and community sources rather than surveys. Indigenous healing has the strictest rule: if a render drifts towards ceremony, use the brand texture instead.
